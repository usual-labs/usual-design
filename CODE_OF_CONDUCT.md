# Code of Conduct

Usual Design welcomes people with different backgrounds, experience, and ways of working. Everyone participating in project issues, pull requests, reviews, and other project-managed spaces is expected to follow these standards. They also apply when someone represents the project publicly.

## Work with care

- Treat people with respect. Explain technical disagreements with examples and evidence.
- Give feedback on the work, and leave room for questions, mistakes, and learning.
- Respect names, pronouns, accessibility needs, and different communication styles.
- Accept a request to stop unwanted interaction. Keep personal information private.
- Take responsibility for your contribution, including work produced with tools or AI assistance.

Harassment, discriminatory abuse, threats, sexual harassment, stalking, impersonation, deliberate intimidation, and publishing someone's private information are unacceptable. Repeated disruption, personal attacks, and retaliation against someone who raises a concern are also unacceptable.

## Reporting a concern

**A confidential conduct-reporting contact has not been configured for this early-stage project.** Before opening public contributions, the repository owner must designate a monitored private contact and an alternate for reports involving that person, verify both routes, and replace this notice with those details.

Do not post sensitive reports or identifying information in a public issue, and do not use a security advisory to report a conduct matter. If a platform's reporting or blocking tools are available, you can use them for immediate protection; those tools are separate from the project's process.

When a project contact is available, share only what is necessary: what happened, where, relevant links or evidence, and any immediate safety or privacy concerns. You do not need to confront the person involved. Maintainers must limit access to reports to people who need it to handle the concern; absolute confidentiality cannot be promised.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for keeping project-managed spaces usable and respectful. They should consider the context, impact, seriousness, and pattern of behavior, and may edit or remove harmful content, request a change in behavior, restrict participation, or remove access.

Actions should be proportionate; serious threats or abuse can require immediate restrictions. A person involved in a report must not decide its outcome. The designated alternate should handle that case. Keep sensitive records private, explain decisions to affected people when safe, and avoid publishing identifying details.

Someone affected by a moderation decision may ask the uninvolved reporting contact to reconsider and provide additional context. Review depends on available uninvolved maintainers; no independent appeals body or response deadline is promised.

This is a project-specific policy. A working private reporting and enforcement arrangement is a prerequisite for public community operations.
